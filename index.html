Key insight — the migration narrative

Linear TV reach declined 11.9%, yet total viewer hours rose 4.5% to 36.22 billion. The audience didn't disappear — it migrated to digital and streaming platforms. This represents a key commercial narrative for FIFA's media rights strategy going into the 2026 edition.

Commercial gap — China

China accounts for 49.8% of all global digital viewing hours (approx. 1,480M hrs), yet contributes only 8.8% of global linear TV viewership. Platforms like Douyin, Migu Video, and CCTV.com are the primary broadcast infrastructure — FIFA's commercial and sponsorship models must urgently reflect this digital-first reality ahead of 2026.

Platform signal — short-form video

The 2022 Final generated 621% more social media engagements than the 2018 Final. Instagram drove 60% of all engagements across the tournament. TikTok grew 116% in total followers (reaching 12M) and surged particularly in the knockout stages. Short-form video is no longer supplementary — it is a primary fan engagement channel requiring dedicated commercial activation.

Opportunity gap — Indonesia

Indonesia recorded 186.4M total engagements (rank 5 globally) and 144.8M digital streaming viewers — nearly matching Brazil (191.9M). Yet it rarely features in FIFA's primary commercial priorities. With 270M+ population, a median age under 30, and a mobile-first digital culture, Indonesia represents one of the highest-ROI underactivated markets ahead of the 2026 World Cup.

Strategic framing

The 2022 data signals a structural shift — not a temporary blip. Linear TV is contracting while digital engagement is compounding at scale. FIFA's commercial model, rights packaging, and sponsorship activation frameworks must pivot accordingly for 2026 and beyond.

Recommended actions by priority
High priority

Repackage digital rights for Asia

China + Indonesia account for ~65% of digital viewing hours (approx. 1,920M hrs) yet are largely served through a handful of OTT platforms with limited FIFA commercial overlay. Develop tiered digital rights bundles including social media licensing, short-form clip rights, and in-stream ad revenue sharing.

High priority

TikTok & short-form monetisation

TikTok grew 116% in followers (reaching 12M) and surged through the final stages. Instagram drove 60% of all engagements (approx. 487M of the 811M total). FIFA should formalise a content licensing framework for short-form creators — this is currently untapped sponsorship inventory worth hundreds of millions.

Medium priority

Indonesia market activation

186M engagements with near-zero dedicated commercial activation. FIFA should develop an Indonesia-specific strategy: local language content production, domestic sponsorship tiers, and digital-first broadcast rights deals well ahead of the 2026 edition.

Medium priority

VOD rights as a new revenue stream

N.C. America has the highest VOD-to-Live ratio of any region: 53M VOD hours vs 202M Live hours (26% VOD share). Europe follows at 12M VOD vs 309M Live (4%). Packaging delayed and on-demand rights separately from live rights — particularly for subscription platforms in the USA and Mexico — could unlock incremental revenue currently bundled or underpriced.

Medium priority

Africa & Middle East depth play

The region grew 91.3% in viewer hours (from 5.17bn to 9.89bn) and recorded the highest hrs/viewer ratio at 2.9 hours. Morocco's semi-final run drove regional surge. High emotional investment makes this an ideal market for premium subscription upsell and live event ancillary revenue.

Monitor

European FTA coverage decline

Europe saw −24.8% in reach (−163.8M viewers) and −28.9% in consumption (−2.8bn hrs). FTA coverage fell sharply — Germany had 21% fewer FTA coverage hours, Japan 33% fewer. The Pay-TV shift reduces organic reach and long-term fan pipeline. FIFA should set minimum FTA reach thresholds in future rights deal structures.

Glossary of terms
FWC — FIFA World Cup
The quadrennial international men's football tournament organised by FIFA. The 2022 edition was held in Qatar (20 Nov – 18 Dec 2022), featuring 32 nations across 64 matches.
MRL — Media Rights Licensee
An organisation granted media rights to broadcast the FIFA World Cup in a specific territory. Data in this report was collected from MRLs via official TV auditing agencies.
FTA — Free-to-Air
Television or digital channels available without a subscription fee. Critical for broad organic reach — FTA coverage hours fell 17.5% globally between 2018 and 2022.
Pay-TV
Channels accessible only via paid subscription (e.g. beIN Sports, Sky, Canal+). Higher ARPU but lower organic reach than FTA. Africa & Middle East had the highest Pay-TV share (77% of coverage hours).
OTT — Over-the-Top
Content delivered directly over the internet, bypassing cable/satellite. Includes streaming apps, broadcaster websites, and IPTV. The primary growth driver in digital reach for 2022.
VOD — Video on Demand
Delayed or on-demand viewing after live broadcast. Includes match replays, highlights, and catch-up services. In 2022, VOD accounted for 316M hours of the 2,969M total digital viewing hours (~10.6%).
Unique Reach / 1-min Reach
Net number of unique individuals who watched an event for at least 1 consecutive minute. Standard broadcast KPI. Global 1-min reach for FWC 2022 was 2.87 billion viewers.
AMA — Average Minute Audience
Sum of per-minute audiences during a broadcast ÷ duration in minutes. Represents how many viewers were watching at any given moment. The 2022 Final AMA was 570.8M viewers.
Viewer Hours
Average audience × broadcast duration in hours. One viewer hour = one person watching one hour. Used to measure total consumption depth. Global TV viewer hours for FWC 2022: 36.22 billion.
TVR% — TV Rating
Audience as a share of all possible TV viewers in a given market. E.g. France's TVR for the 2022 Final was 44.0%, meaning 44% of France's total TV universe watched.
DAU — Daily Active Users
Unique users engaging with a digital platform on a given day. FIFA's avg DAU across all platforms was 18.9M across the 29-day tournament window (20 Nov – 18 Dec 2022).
O&O Digital
Owned & Operated platforms — broadcaster websites, apps, and OTT services run directly by the MRL. In 2022, O&O platforms accounted for 90.4% of all digital viewing hours (vs 9.6% on social media).
PSE — Publicis Sport & Entertainment
The agency commissioned by FIFA to source, verify, and analyse audience and broadcast data across all territories. Co-producer of the 2022 Global Engagement & Audience Detailed Report.
Competing vs Non-competing markets
Competing markets had a national team in the 2022 tournament (e.g. Brazil, France). Non-competing markets (e.g. China, India) had no team — yet China ranked 1st in total engagement with 1,161.3M fans.
